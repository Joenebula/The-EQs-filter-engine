ParaEQ user manual

ParaEQ is a 24-band parametric EQ plugin (VST3, 64-bit Windows) made for Cubase. It has a large interactive EQ graph, a real-time spectrum analyser, dynamic EQ on any bell or shelf, left/right/mid/side processing per band, presets, A/B compare and full automation.

Version 1.0 · Vendor "Home Studio" · Personal use.

1. Installing

With the installer (recommended)

  1. Close Cubase.
  2. Download ParaEQ-Setup-Windows-x64.exe and run it. Windows may warn that the app is from an unknown publisher (the installer is not code-signed). Click More info > Run anyway.
  3. Click through the installer. It puts:
  4. the plugin in C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3\ParaEQ.vst3
  5. this manual in C:\Program Files\Home Studio\ParaEQ, with a Start menu shortcut.
  6. Start Cubase. ParaEQ appears in the plugin list under EQ, vendor Home Studio. If it doesn't, open Studio > VST Plug-in Manager and click Rescan.

By hand (zip file)

Unzip ParaEQ-VST3-Windows-x64.zip and copy the ParaEQ.vst3 folder into C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3\. Then rescan in Cubase as above.

Updating

Close Cubase and run the new installer (or copy the new ParaEQ.vst3 over the old one). Your projects and presets are kept. Projects saved with an older ParaEQ open with the same sound.

Uninstalling

Windows Settings > Apps > ParaEQ > Uninstall. Your own presets stay in %APPDATA%\Home Studio\ParaEQ\Presets; delete that folder if you don't want them.

The window can be resized from its bottom-right corner. Its size is saved with the project.

3. Working with bands

Adding and moving

  • Click an empty spot on the graph to add a band there. Near 20 Hz you get a low cut, near 20 kHz a high cut, anywhere else a bell.
  • Drag a dot: left/right changes the frequency, up/down the gain. Hold Shift for fine moves.
  • Mouse wheel over a dot changes the Q (width). Higher Q = narrower.
  • Double-click a dot to reset its gain to 0 dB.
  • Right-click a dot for type, slope, channels, enable, solo, copy, paste and delete.
  • Delete key removes the selected band.

The band panel

The strip at the bottom shows every setting of the selected band:

  • Type, Slope, Channels (see below).
  • Dynamic switch and its four knobs (section 4).
  • Freq, Gain, Q knobs.
  • On (bypass just this band), Solo, Delete.

Double-click a knob to reset it. Drag up/down or left/right to turn it.

Filter types

Type Use Gain Slope
Bell Boost or cut around a frequency yes -
Low Shelf Boost or cut everything below yes 6 or 12 dB/oct
High Shelf Boost or cut everything above yes 6 or 12 dB/oct
High-pass (low cut) Remove rumble and low end - 6 to 96 dB/oct
Low-pass (high cut) Remove highs - 6 to 96 dB/oct
Notch Remove one narrow frequency (hum, ring) - -
Band-pass Keep only a region - 6 to 48 dB/oct

Channels (per band)

Setting The band works on
Stereo Both channels together (normal)
Left / Right One channel only
Mid What's in the centre (vocals, kick, bass, snare)
Side What's at the sides (width, reverb, wide guitars)

Bands that aren't Stereo show a small letter (L, R, M, S) next to their dot and a dashed curve. The white combined curve treats all bands as if they were Stereo, so for L/R/M/S bands it's a guide, not exact.

Solo

Solo (panel button or right-click) plays only the frequency region of that band. It's a quick way to find a harsh or muddy area. Click again to stop. Solo is a listening aid; it isn't saved or automated.

Copy and paste

Right-click a dot and choose Copy band, or select it and press Ctrl+C. Ctrl+V pastes it as a new band; right-click another dot and choose Paste onto this band to replace it. This also works between two ParaEQs in the same project.

(Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V only reach ParaEQ when its window has keyboard focus; click the graph first. The right-click menu always works.)

4. Dynamic EQ

A dynamic band only acts when its frequency region gets loud. It works on bells and shelves.

  1. Select a bell or shelf and click Dynamic in the band panel.
  2. Threshold: the level (in dBFS of that region) where it starts to act. The live bar under the Dynamic button shows the gain change as it happens; set the threshold so it moves on the loud parts only.
  3. Range: the most the gain may move. Negative turns the band down when loud (taming harsh "s" sounds, a boomy note). Positive turns it up when loud.
  4. Attack: how fast it reacts (0.1 to 200 ms). Release: how fast it lets go (5 ms to 2 s).

The band's normal Gain still applies: the dynamics move away from it. For example, Gain +3 dB with Range -6 dB gives +3 dB on quiet parts and down to -3 dB on loud parts.

On the graph a dynamic band shows:

  • a bar from its dot to where its range ends,
  • a dotted curve for the full range,
  • live, the curve it is applying right now (shaded).

How it works: each dynamic band listens to its own frequency region (a band-pass at the band's frequency and Q for a bell, low or high frequencies for a shelf). L/R/M/S bands listen only to their own channel. Above the threshold the gain moves 1 dB for every 2 dB the region goes over, with a soft knee, up to the range.

5. Spectrum analyser

The analyser draws what's playing behind the EQ curves. The filled shape is the output (after the EQ), the grey line the input (before it). The scale on the left is dBFS (0 at the top, -90 at the bottom).

Settings (Analyser button):

Setting Choices
Show Off, Post-EQ, Pre + Post, Pre-EQ
Resolution 2048 to 16384 points. Higher = more detail in the lows, slower
Speed How fast the display falls back
Smoothing Off, 1/12, 1/6 or 1/3 octave
Tilt 0, 3 or 4.5 dB/oct (makes typical music look flat; display only)
Opacity 25 to 100 %
Peak hold A thin line showing recent peaks

The analyser only runs while the window is open.

6. Presets

Click the preset name for the menu:

  • Factory presets: 14 starting points (vocals, drums, bass, guitar, master, effects). Load one and adjust it to your track.
  • My presets: presets you saved.
  • Save (overwrite the current user preset), Save as... (new name), Rename..., Delete (goes to the Recycle Bin).
  • Import... / Export...: .paraeq files, to share or back up.
  • Show presets folder: %APPDATA%\Home Studio\ParaEQ\Presets.

A preset holds all bands, dynamics and the IN/OUT gain. It doesn't change Bypass, the window size or the analyser settings. Loading a preset can be undone with Undo.

Cubase's own preset menu (at the top of the plugin window) also works; it saves everything, including the window and analyser settings.

7. A/B compare

Click A to switch to B. The first time, B starts as a copy of A. Make changes, then switch back and forth to compare. Copy A to B (or B to A) is in the preset menu. Both settings are saved with the project.

8. Using ParaEQ in Cubase

  • Insert it on any mono or stereo audio track, group, FX channel or the stereo out.
  • Automation: every setting can be automated. Use Write (W) on the track and move a knob or a dot. The parameters are named "Band 1 Freq", "Band 1 Gain", and so on, grouped by band.
  • Latency: zero. ParaEQ adds no delay, so it's safe for recording and live monitoring.
  • 64-bit processing: supported (Studio > Studio Setup > Audio System > Processing Precision). ParaEQ works in 64-bit inside either way.
  • Sample rates: anything from 22.05 to 192 kHz. Bands above what the sample rate allows are limited automatically.
  • CPU: very light. A fully loaded instance (24 bands, half of them dynamic) measured about 1.5 % of one CPU core on the test machine.
  • Projects save all settings, the preset name, A/B, window size and analyser settings.

10. Troubleshooting

Problem Try this
ParaEQ isn't in the plugin list Check C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3\ParaEQ.vst3 exists, then Studio > VST Plug-in Manager > Rescan. Make sure Cubase is 64-bit.
Cubase lists it as blocked In the Plug-in Manager, select ParaEQ in the blocklist and click "Reactivate".
No sound change Is Bypass on? Is the band's On button on? Is the band set to Side on a mono source?
The analyser is flat It only shows while audio plays and the window is open. Check Analyser > Show isn't Off.
Ctrl+Z undoes something in Cubase instead Click inside the ParaEQ window first, or use the Undo button.
Output meter shows red The output went over 0 dBFS. Lower OUT or the boosts.

11. Known limitations

  • Automation is applied once per audio block and glided over about 20 ms, so it's smooth but not sample-accurate.
  • Dynamic EQ has no look-ahead, so the very start of a sharp hit can pass before a band reacts. The ratio (1 dB per 2 dB) and knee are fixed.
  • No external sidechain input.
  • The combined curve is exact only for Stereo bands.
  • The installer and plugin are not code-signed, so Windows shows a warning the first time.
